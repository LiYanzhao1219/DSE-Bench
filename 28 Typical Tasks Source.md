Evidence Levels A and B are defined as follows:

> **Level A (Direct Support, 24 tasks):** At least one listed source explicitly describes a core technical activity, tool function, or real-world engineering example directly corresponding to the task name.

> **Level B (Synthesized, 4 tasks):** The listed literature supports constituent activities of the task, but its full scope or name is synthesized from multiple sources. The boundaries of this synthesis are described in Section 2.

Task names follow the abbreviated forms used in Table 1 of the paper. Level A evidence may be based on a clearly documented technical function or a representative engineering example and cannot automatically be generalized to all mission scenarios. For example, `mdiscal` in T22 is a calibration tool for the MDIS camera aboard the MESSENGER mission, while T04 uses near-terminator orbit design around a small body as a technical example of “Orbital design around target bodies.”

## 2. Synthesis Rationale and Evidence Boundaries for Level B Tasks

| Task | Content Explicitly Supported by the Literature | Aspects Synthesized in the Task Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T01 Small-body target assessment** | JPL's Small-Body Mission-Design Tool supports the analysis of mission opportunities, trajectory reachability, and associated maneuver conditions for candidate small bodies. | Combining engineering reachability, scientific value, and observation conditions into an overall target assessment; the cited tool alone does not provide a complete method for evaluating scientific value. |
| **T05 ΔV and propellant budgeting** | NASA GSFC's GDC Orbit Primer explicitly presents the relationship between ΔV and propellant mass, while NASA's navigation tutorial discusses trajectory maneuvers and their required ΔV. | Combining maneuver ΔV calculations, propellant requirements, and engineering margins into a joint budgeting task; the cited example does not constitute a universal propellant budgeting procedure. |
| **T08 Science payload selection and configuration** | NASA's Science Traceability Matrix establishes links among science objectives, measurement requirements, and instrument requirements; the Mercury Lander concept study presents a payload comprising 11 instruments. | Generalizing the objective–requirement–instrument traceability framework and the mission example into science payload selection and configuration; the sources are not claimed to provide a unified payload configuration optimization algorithm. |
| **T25 Spectral analysis and material identification** | NASA's CRISM materials explicitly describe identifying Martian minerals from spectral features, while the PDS CRISM TRDR documentation describes calibrated spectral data products. | Combining spectral data processing and spectral-based material identification into one typical task; the two sources support data processing and scientific interpretation separately and do not provide a single end-to-end algorithm. |
